## hyd-market
Hyderabad has the second-densest student market in India, after Bengaluru. The official AISHE 2023-24 report counts 17.87 lakh higher-education students in Telangana. The state's GER is 46.6, against 30.0 for India. Telangana has 56 colleges per lakh people aged 18-23, second only to Karnataka. Hyderabad district alone has 465 colleges, 4th of all districts in India, and Ranga Reddy is 9th with 338. Mumbai and Delhi have no district in the top 10. Engineering students are concentrated and lean heavily to tech. 61% of Telangana's engineering colleges (107 of 175) are in Medchal-Malkajgiri, Ranga Reddy and Hyderabad. JNTUH-affiliated private colleges alone take 87,804 students a year. About 83,600 of roughly 1.17 lakh engineering seats are in CSE and allied branches. On the degree side, Osmania University's area filled about 99,000 of 1.98 lakh DOST seats in 2025-26, close to half of Telangana's degree seats. Census 2027 house-listing gives a preliminary count of 1.42 crore people inside the Outer Ring Road. Telangana's 9.39 lakh IT workers and about 515 GCCs (global capability centres) give a large secondary audience of young professionals. Hyderabad (UA) is officially an X-class (Tier-1) city under the 7th CPC HRA order. It is also cheaper than Bengaluru, Delhi and Mumbai (Mercer 2024 ranks: Hyderabad 202, Bengaluru 195, Delhi 164, Mumbai 136). The market is huge next to a 2,500-3,000 visitor target, so the real limits are conversion, travel to peripheral engineering clusters, and clashes with big fests such as IIT-H's Elan & nVision, which claimed 30,000+ students on Jan 9-11, 2026.

### Implications
- Tier-1 case for Hyderabad: officially an X-class city (7th CPC), 4th in India for colleges per district (465) plus Ranga Reddy at 9th (338), Telangana GER 46.6 against 30.0 nationally, and cheaper than Bengaluru, Delhi and Mumbai (Mercer 2024).
- The market is not the constraint. A 2,500-3,000 visitor goal is a tiny share of an estimated 7-9 lakh metro students, so the plan should optimise channel efficiency (referrals, college partnerships) and registration-to-attendance conversion rather than reach.
- Geo-concentrate on the engineering belts. 107 of 175 engineering colleges are in Medchal-Malkajgiri, Ranga Reddy and Hyderabad, so ambassador recruitment and geo-fenced paid social should centre on these clusters, and the venue should be reachable from both northern and western/southern belts.
- Content should lean to tech. About 72% of 2025-26 engineering seats are CSE and allied (83,622 of 1,16,877), so hackathons, AI workshops and GCC/IT hiring zones are strong acquisition hooks and sponsor magnets (515 GCCs, 9.39 lakh IT workers).
- College partnerships are high-leverage. The average JNTUH private college has about 675 freshers a year, and OU's area admits about 99,000 degree students a year, so a few dozen formal partnerships can cover a large share of the pool cheaply, consistent with last edition's low cost per visitor for referrals and partnerships.
- Transport is a show-up lever. The metro covers only 69 km and many engineering colleges sit on the periphery, so college-pickup shuttles or group-travel incentives should sit inside the conversion funnel to lift the roughly 69% registrant show-up rate.
- Plan around the competing fests. IIT-H's Elan & nVision claimed 30,000+ students in January 2026, so avoid its window or co-promote. Residential flagship campuses are better as content and credibility partners than as volume sources.
- Young professionals are an optional secondary segment, best served with a ticketed weekend evening slot without diluting the student-first verified-visitor metric.

## fest-landscape
Hyderabad's college-fest season is crowded and moves around from year to year. In 2026, Elan & nVision (IIT Hyderabad) moved from late February to 9-11 January and claimed 30,000+ students. The Hyderabad Literary Festival ran 24-26 January, Felicity (IIIT Hyderabad) 13-15 February, CBIT's Shruthi ended around 21 February and Pearl (BITS Hyderabad) held its main pro-show on 22 February. Numaish runs right through this window (about 23.4 lakh visitors in 2026, with 14-29-year-olds the largest group). The big Hyderabad fests normally have free general entry: Elan 2025 said 'free entry for everyone', and Infinium and HLF were also free. Paid tickets are kept for star pro-nites: Pearl sold public tickets from Rs 500 on BookMyShow, and Seedhe Maut's Pearl 2026 show sold out with a reported 15,000+ crowd. There is strong demand in Hyderabad for big music acts (1.5 lakh+ ticket requests for Anirudh's 30,000-seat Gachibowli show), and the same mid-tier indie acts play several Hyderabad fests within weeks of each other, so a headliner's novelty runs out fast. Two recent incidents show that a growth plan must control entry and verify every visitor. Mood Indigo 2025 cancelled all pre-registrations and shut out outsiders after crowds built up at the gate. A Malla Reddy University film event in August 2026 had permission for about 7,000 people, drew about 20,000, and led to injuries and two police cases. Headline footfall figures are mostly organisers' estimates and count the host college's own students: Riviera expected 18,000+ participants but only about 1,000+ from other colleges. So benchmarks for unique external visitors have to be scaled down sharply. National fests grow their reach through large campus-ambassador programmes with leaderboard rewards (Techfest: 50,000 openings, top-10 free accommodation, top-100 recommendation letters; Mood Indigo: 500 openings plus free accommodation and special entry). This lines up with CampusFest 2025, where student referrals cost Rs 69 per actual visitor against Rs 274 for paid social.

### Implications
- Calendar is the first growth lever. In 2026 the Hyderabad fest season ran from Elan (Jan 9-11) through HLF (Jan 24-26), Felicity (Feb 13-15) and CBIT Shruthi and Pearl (~Feb 19-22), with Numaish running all of January to mid-February. A fest in a free weekend, after Sankranti and pre-finals (Jan 13-23), avoids splitting the same student crowd.
- Free entry is the norm in Hyderabad, so charging for general entry would hurt reach. Keep paid or gated access only for the pro-nite, and use the free pass mainly to capture verified registrations (name, college, phone) that feed reminders and referrals.
- Measure success by unique verified check-ins, not registrations. Mood Indigo 2025 (registrations cancelled mid-fest) and Malla Reddy 2026 (about 20,000 people against a 7,000 permission) show that unmanaged demand creates safety, legal and reputation risks. QR scanning at the gate makes the metric and crowd safety the same system.
- Competitor footfall figures are inflated by internal students (Riviera: about 1,000 external out of 18,000+). The pitch should openly re-base benchmarks to external unique visitors. This makes the target credible and sets the candidate apart.
- The cheapest way to grow is a campus-ambassador and referral leaderboard tied to verified attendance. National fests run tiered, almost cash-free reward systems (certificates, recommendation letters, free accommodation or entry for the top 10 to 100), which fits CampusFest's Rs 69 per visitor for referrals against Rs 274 for paid social.
- Content has to work for reels. Numaish's youth surge (14-29 now the largest segment) was linked to reels, and Felicity's Lulu Mall flashmob drew 750+ people. Offline activations at youth hubs (malls, Numaish, Metro stations), filmed for reels, are a cheap way to turn awareness into registrations.
- Competitions with real prize pools reliably bring in participants (Elan Rs 16 lakh, Riviera Rs 25 lakh, Infinium Rs 12.2 lakh, Sintillashunz Rs 5 lakh+). A sponsor-funded hackathon or gaming tournament (BGMI, as at Comic Con) can bring whole teams and their friends. Each participant should be counted as a visitor only on check-in.
- Pick a headliner who adds novelty. Hyderabad fests recycle the same indie and comedy acts within weeks, while South Indian film-music demand is very large (1.5 lakh+ requests for 30,000 seats). A regional star or a fresh act gives people a reason to come and to post about it, but needs police permission sized to the realistic peak crowd.

## channel-costs
India is one of the cheapest paid-media markets in the world. Vendor benchmarks put Instagram Reels CPM at Rs 45-140, against Rs 150-350 for Feed. Superads data shows India's Meta CPM and CPC running about 90% below the global median, with the lowest month in February 2026. A late-Feb to mid-March 2027 fest therefore buys its paid reach in a cheap window. India has 570 million Instagram users (Oct 2026), and 37.3% are 18-24, so reach is not the limit. Converting attention into people who actually turn up is. That is why last edition's paid social cost Rs 274 per actual visitor, against Rs 69 for student referrals. WhatsApp is the cheapest channel for nudging people to show up. Utility messages cost Rs 0.115 and marketing messages Rs 0.8631 per delivered message before vendor fees and GST. Meta's official docs also say all messages are free for up to 7 days after a user arrives through a Click-to-WhatsApp ad, so 'CTWA ad, then WhatsApp registration and reminder flow' is a high-leverage design. For creators, one macro influencer (Rs 2-12 lakh per Reel nationally) would use up the whole ~Rs 3 lakh budget. Hyderabad nano creators run Rs 3,000-8,000 per Reel and micro creators Rs 8,000-25,000. PTI/Reelax data (H1 2026) shows regional micro-creators getting 2-3x the engagement of metro macro creators at about one-tenth the cost per post. Referral and campus-ambassador programmes are mostly paid in non-cash rewards (certificates, merch, free passes, accommodation, internships), unlocked at tiered thresholds (e.g. IIT Ropar: 100+ registrations earns a free pass; Vignan Mahotsav in Andhra Pradesh: 50 registrations earns free accommodation and meals). That keeps cash cost per attendee very low. Western referral benchmarks (Extole: ~19% of customers refer, ~13% of referred friends convert; Ticket Fairy: festivals report 20-30% more ticket revenue from referral) support scaling the cheapest channel from last year. Email is weak in India for generic sends (MoEngage Media & Entertainment broadcast open rate 17.16%, click-to-open rate 2.19%) but strong when personalised on behaviour (33.25% open, 24.55% click-to-open), so use it only for triggered sequences. Most cost figures come from vendor and agency blogs, so treat them as planning ranges, not audited data.

### Implications
- Rebuild paid social as Reels/Stories Click-to-WhatsApp campaigns: Reels CPM is about 1/3 of Feed, and the 7-day free WhatsApp window makes follow-up messages free. The aim is to cut paid cost per actual visitor from Rs 274 toward Rs 150-200.
- Make WhatsApp the show-up engine: utility-template confirmations, QR passes and T-3d/T-1d/morning-of reminders cost about Rs 0.24 each all-in, so lifting show-up from ~69% by even a few points costs less than any acquisition channel.
- Put referral and campus ambassadors at the centre of the plan. Both rely on non-cash tiered rewards (passes, merch, accommodation, certificates, internships) and should be tied to verified check-ins, not registrations, to remove fake or low-intent sign-ups.
- Use many Hyderabad/Telugu nano and micro creators instead of any macro creator. Screen for fake followers (about 1 in 4 profiles fail) and pay partly in passes or barter.
- Treat email and SMS as support channels: generic email gets about 17% open and 2% CTOR in Indian M&E, so send only personalised, triggered messages, and keep SMS as the fallback for people without WhatsApp opt-in.
- Schedule paid bursts in Jan-Mar, when Indian Meta CPMs are seasonally cheap, and avoid clashing with Hyderabad competitor fests such as IIT Hyderabad's Elan & nVision (early January).
- Skip paid stalls and sponsorship at other colleges (Rs 15,000-2 lakh each). Run college partnerships in kind, through club co-hosted events and pass quotas.

## show-rates
The strongest measured benchmark comes from PheedLoop's check-in data on 1,070+ live events (2026). At free events the median no-show rate is about 28%, against about 17% at paid events. At free events with 500 or more attendees it is about 24%, so roughly 76% of registrants attend. Last year CampusFest converted only about 69% of registrants (1,925 of 2,800) into visitors, below the large-free-event median. Lifting show-up is therefore probably cheaper than buying more registrations. Price commitment works mainly at the event level. Within the same event, people who paid show up only 2 to 4 points more often than people who did not. The one deposit case found, Basecamp's refundable $100 deposit, cut no-shows from 55% to 10%, but it is a single US anecdote. Reminders are well proven. In randomized trials, text reminders cut no-shows by about 25% (RR 0.75), two reminders beat one (4.4% missed vs 5.3–5.8%), and messages saying the slot is reserved for the person ('ownership' wording) or stating what is lost by not coming did best. Plan-making prompts (asking when, how and with whom someone will come) raised turnout by 4.1 points overall and 9.1 points for people deciding alone. Showing people that others can see whether they came raised turnout by 8.1 points, so a friend-group or college-squad mechanic is the natural fit. Distance is a real cost: one extra mile cut in-person voting by 2 to 12 points across studies. Hyderabad Metro's last train has been at 11 PM since November 2025, which caps how late the night events can run unless shuttles are added. On tools, Townscript has shut down, KonfHub and Luma publish fees and offer QR check-in plus WhatsApp/SMS reminders, and District and BookMyShow do not publish organiser commissions.

### Implications
- The biggest cheap win is moving registrants to visitors, not adding registrations. Each point of show-up on about 3,200 registrations is about 32 extra verified visitors, and closing the gap from 69% to 76% adds about 220 visitors at almost no media cost.
- Build a reminder system with measurement built in: WhatsApp utility templates on a 4-touch cadence, 'reserved for you' wording on the pass, loss framing at T-1 ('release your spot if you can't come; 300 are waitlisted'), and a 10% no-reminder holdout so the lift can be proven to RISA-style reviewers.
- Add a plan-making step at registration: when you are coming, how you will travel (metro station and route) and who you are coming with. It is supported by randomized evidence (+4.1 pp, +9.1 pp for solo deciders) and doubles as data for transport planning.
- Make squads the default unit: group registration links, squad check-in visible to friends and inter-college check-in leaderboards. Social visibility (+8.1 pp in turnout) is likely to work well with Indian student peer groups and the campus ambassador network.
- Use a mixed pass design: a free general pass to maximise reach, plus a token or refundable 'Priority/Pro-show' pass returned as a credit at entry. Run it as a test cell, because the evidence says event-level commitment matters more than individual payment.
- Treat geography as a lever: target colleges near metro lines and the venue first, run shuttles from far clusters, put travel time in reminders and end evening shows before the 11 PM last metro.
- Calibrate overbooking and waitlists per zone and segment (capacity ÷ expected show rate), using the 2025 confirmed-to-visit rate (1,925/2,500 = 77%) as the starting value, and release waitlist spots in batches in the final week.
- Tooling: Luma (free events, multi-channel reminders, QR check-in, approval or waitlist) or KonfHub (UPI-native, WhatsApp at INR 1 per message, check-in app) for free registration; Unstop for competitions; District or BookMyShow only for paid headline-night reach after getting commission quotes in writing; Townscript has shut down.

### Implications
- The installed dependencies already match the npm latest versions, so no upgrade is needed. All new code must use AI SDK 7 APIs, because v5/v6 snippets (stepCountIs, system, needsApproval, result.toUIMessageStreamResponse) are renamed or deprecated.
- Replace any gemini-2.5-flash or gemini-2.5-pro reference before December 1, 2026. Auto-routing would silently switch models, changing behaviour and cost mid-review.
- Tool approval (toolApproval + addToolApprovalResponse) is a strong PM story: the AI suggests budget shifts or campaign actions, and a human approves before anything is written to Neon.
- Set stopWhen explicitly. Otherwise the assistant runs a tool and then stops without a final answer, which would undermine the demo.
- Gemini's pricing for gemini-3.8-flash doubles on January 1, 2027, which falls inside the festival planning window. Any 'production cost' estimate should use the 2027 rate.
- Co-locating functions (sin1) with Neon (Singapore) and warming the database before a live demo avoids the 5-minute scale-to-zero cold start and the cross-Pacific latency of the default iad1 region.
- Keep the tool set small, with flat schemas and short enums, and validate lengths and ranges server-side with zod. Gemini strips length constraints and rejects schemas that are too complex.
